# EMA exit rules: take profit, trailing stop, break-even

Status: built, every rule OFF. Nothing here changes a trade until the operator sets a value, and the owner has said no value is to be set until a sealed test supports it. The default EMA trade exits exactly as before: the opposite crossover or its fixed 2 ATR stop.

## Switches (all default 0 = off)
| Variable | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `EMA_TAKE_PROFIT_R` | Exit when the price reaches entry +/- this many R. R = the distance from the entry to the INITIAL stop (`trades.initial_stop_price`). |
| `EMA_TRAIL_TRIGGER_R` | Once the trade has been up this many R (best high since entry for a long), trail the protective stop. |
| `EMA_TRAIL_ATR` | Trail distance, in ATR of the candle (default 2, clamped 0.5 to 10). Only read when the trigger is on. |
| `EMA_BREAKEVEN_R` | Once the trade has been up this many R, move the stop to the entry price. Its own switch. |
Unset, empty, malformed, zero, negative or non-finite is 0 (off); a value that is on is clamped to 0.25 to 50. A typo can never switch a rule on.

## The rules (one pure module, `ema_exits.py`, used by the live path, the paper path and the replay)
Over the CLOSED candles that started after the entry: the stop starts at the initial stop; take profit exits when a candle's high (long) or low (short) reaches the level; trailing sets the stop to the best price minus `EMA_TRAIL_ATR` x ATR, ratcheted (never lowered); break-even sets it to at least the entry price. A level a candle earns applies from the NEXT candle. If one candle touches both the stop and the take profit the STOP wins. A candle that opens beyond a level fills at its open (the worse price). Take profit is always on the profit side: it can never fire on the loss side (a test sweeps prices for both directions).

## Live: how each exit happens
* **Take profit is MONITORED each round; there is no exchange-side take-profit order.** Reason: a second resting order interacts with the protective stop (Binance's handling of two closing orders is UNVERIFIED here), and the protective stop is what must never be put at risk. When a candle after the entry, the still-forming candle or the live price has reached the level, the trade exits through `lifecycle.execute_exit`, the only live exit path, as a market exit (so the fill can be worse than the target; the P&L line shows the real fill).
* **If the bot is down** nothing exits at the target while it is down, but the protective stop stays at the exchange. When the bot is back, the touch is found from the candles it missed and the trade exits at the market (a test: touched in hour 1, price back at 95 two hours later, it still exits and says so).
* **If the exit order is rejected** the outcome is `execute_exit`'s existing one: the trade shows `closing`, then `attention` (`exit_failed`) with one customer alert; the reconciler keeps working it. Nothing new was added there.
* **If the position is already closed** (the exchange stop fired) the trade is reconciled first and closed as `exchange_stop_triggered`; the rules never run on a trade that is not open and protected, and no second exit is sent.
* **Trailing stop and break-even** use `lifecycle.move_stop` (the Donchian trail's mechanism): the NEW stop is placed and read back resting before the trade records it and before the old one is cancelled; only favourable moves, only on the losing side of the price; a refused or unverifiable new stop keeps the old one and tells the customer once. The position is never without a stop. If a closed candle already went through the earned level (the bot missed it), the trade exits through `execute_exit` with that reason (`trailing_stop` or `break_even_stop`).
* Applies to EMA trades only (`strategy_id` empty or `ema_cross_atr_v1`) and only when the trade has an initial stop (every live trade and, from this change, every paper trade does). Donchian and RSI trades never get these rules. One code path for every account, single or multi position.
* PAPER trades follow the same rules: the stop ratchets, take profit exits at the target price, close reasons `take_profit`, `trailing_stop`, `break_even_stop`.

## Effect on the paper replay (a mechanics demonstration, not a claim)
`python -m mba_bot.paper_replay --trades ... --klines ... [--take-profit-r X] [--trail-trigger-r X --trail-atr Y] [--breakeven-r X]` now also replays the rules on the same trades and prints the average R with and without them, how many trades were better, worse or the same, and the exits by reason.
No paper-trade CSV exists in the build environment (it lives on the server), so the PR shows the tool on trades GENERATED by running the bot's own EMA entry and exit rules (20/50, 2 ATR stop, crossover exit) over BTC and ETH 1h candles from Binance's public archive, November 2025 to October 2026: 185 trades. Example values fixed before looking, not tuned. Average R on the 178 comparable trades without rules +0.342; take profit at 3R +0.007; trailing from 1.5R by 2 ATR +0.064; break-even at 1R +0.382; all three +0.066. Hourly candles, no fees, no slippage, one year. Read it as: take profit and a trailing stop cut the average of this trend-following strategy on these trades, break-even slightly raised it, and none of it is evidence for or against switching anything on.

## Rollback
Set the value to 0 (or unset it) and restart. A trade that already had its stop moved keeps that stop (it is a normal protective stop); the rules do nothing further.

## Not proven
The fake exchange stands in for Binance. Nothing here shows any rule makes money.
